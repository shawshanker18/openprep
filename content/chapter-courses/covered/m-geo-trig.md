## Quick recap

From the Trigonometric Functions chapter you know radians and the unit circle. This route assumes you are comfortable with them and goes to the SAT uses: reading coordinates off the unit circle, signs by quadrant, converting between degrees and radians, and arc length.

## What the SAT adds

- **Unit circle coordinates.** The point at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. Use the reference angle for the size and the quadrant for the sign.
- **Sign by quadrant.** In quadrant II sine is positive and cosine is negative. In quadrant III both are negative. In quadrant IV cosine is positive and sine is negative.
- **Complementary angles.** $\sin x^\circ = \cos(90 - x)^\circ$, so if $\sin x^\circ = \cos y^\circ$ then $x + y = 90$.
- **Arc length.** On a circle of radius $r$, an angle of $\theta$ radians cuts an arc of length $r\theta$.

## Worked example

If $\sin\theta = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\theta$ is in quadrant II, what is $\cos\theta$?

Sketch a right triangle with opposite side $3$ and hypotenuse $5$, so the adjacent side is $4$. Cosine is negative in quadrant II, so $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{4}{5}$.

Find the point on the unit circle at $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

The reference angle is $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, where cosine is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and sine is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. In quadrant II the $x$-coordinate is negative, so the point is $\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$.

A circle has radius $10$ and a central angle of $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$. The arc length is $10 \cdot \dfrac{2\pi}{5} = 4\pi$.

## Common traps

- Getting the right size but the wrong sign. Say the quadrant out loud before you write the answer.
- Using arc length $r\theta$ with $\theta$ in degrees. Convert to radians first.
- Leaving a calculator in the wrong angle mode.

## Shortcuts and calculator checks

Multiply degrees by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$ to get radians. In Desmos the angle mode is under the settings wrench: check it matches the question before you evaluate a sine or cosine.
