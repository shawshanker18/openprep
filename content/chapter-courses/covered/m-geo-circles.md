## Quick recap

You know the standard equation of a circle, $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, from the Conic Sections chapter. This route focuses on the SAT versions: equations that are not yet in standard form, and finding the radius from a point on the circle.

## What the SAT adds

- **Completing the square.** An equation like $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 10y + 18 = 0$ has to be rewritten before you can read the center and radius.
- **Shortcut for the center.** For $x^2 + y^2 + ax + by + c = 0$ the center is $\left(-\dfrac{a}{2}, -\dfrac{b}{2}\right)$ and $r^2 = \left(\dfrac{a}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 - c$.
- **Radius from two points.** The distance from the center to any point on the circle is $r$.

## Worked example

Find the center and radius of $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 10y + 18 = 0$.

Group the terms: $(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 10y) = -18$. Add $9$ and $25$ to both sides to complete the squares: $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = -18 + 9 + 25 = 16$. The center is $(3, -5)$ and the radius is $4$.

A circle has center $(-2, 1)$ and passes through $(1, 5)$. Write its equation.

The horizontal gap is $3$ and the vertical gap is $4$, so $r^2 = 3^2 + 4^2 = 25$. The equation is $(x + 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 25$.

## Common traps

- Reading the center with the wrong signs. $(x + 2)$ means the center's $x$-coordinate is $-2$.
- Giving $r^2$ when the question asks for the radius, or the other way round.
- Adding the completing-the-square numbers on one side only. Add them to both sides.

## Shortcuts and calculator checks

Paste the equation into Desmos. The circle appears, and clicking the center or an axis crossing shows exact coordinates. Then check your radius against the picture.
