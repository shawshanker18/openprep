---
title: Solving any equation by graphing
order: 3
expressions: ['y=\sqrt{x+7}', 'y=x-5']
---
Any equation in one variable can be solved by graphing **each side as its own curve** and finding where they meet.

**Try it:** to solve $\sqrt{x + 7} = x - 5$, graph $y = \sqrt{x+7}$ and $y = x - 5$. They cross only at $x = 9$.

- This automatically avoids **extraneous solutions**: squaring both sides by hand gives $x = 2$ too, but the graphs show it isn't real.
- Another way: move everything to one side and graph $y = (\text{left}) - (\text{right})$. The solutions are the $x$-intercepts.

**On the test:** use this for radical, absolute-value, and messy rational equations, where algebra is slow or error-prone.
