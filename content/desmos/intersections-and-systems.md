title: Intersections and systems

The solution of a system of equations is the point where their graphs cross.

**Try it:** the two lines $3x + 2y = 12$ and $x - y = -1$ are loaded. Click where they cross, and GeoGebra shows $(2, 3)$, so $x = 2$ and $y = 3$.

- You don't need to rearrange into $y = \ldots$ form. GeoGebra graphs equations in any form.
- **No intersection** (parallel lines) means no solution. **The same line twice** means infinitely many solutions.
- For a line and a parabola, count the crossing points to answer "how many solutions?" questions.

**On the test:** if the question asks for $x + y$ or $2x - y$, find the intersection first, then compute. There is no need to solve it symbolically.
