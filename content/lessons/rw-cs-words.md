## What this skill is

Words in Context questions give you a sentence or short text with a blank and ask, "Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?" You choose the word that fits the meaning the rest of the text sets up. The words range from everyday ("reserved," "withstand") to harder vocabulary ("ephemeral," "derivative").

## Learn the idea

**Try this first.** "The guide's instructions were so lucid that even beginners followed them easily." What does *lucid* mean? Use the sentence, not a memory of the word.

**Let's work it out.**

1. Find the clue. The sentence says that even beginners followed the instructions easily. Why is that a clue? Because the result, easy following, tells you what kind of instructions they were.
2. Predict a meaning from the clue before looking at any choice. Instructions that beginners can follow easily are clear.
3. Test the prediction against the answer choices. "Clear" fits. "Complicated" contradicts the clue, and "hidden" has nothing to do with it.
4. Why is the check important? Many words have more than one meaning, and the clue picks the one this text uses.

**Your turn.** "After the long climb, the hikers were ravenous." What does *ravenous* mean, and what clue gives it away?

**Answer:** It means very hungry. The clue is "after the long climb," which makes hunger the likely result of effort.

## Formulas and rules

**Key rules**

| Rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| Find the clue | Definition, contrast, or cause and effect in the sentence decides the meaning. |
| Predict first | Form a meaning from the clue before reading the choices. |
| Check the degree | Two choices can point the right way, but only one fits the exact strength. |

| Clue word or mark | Blank should... |
|---|---|
| colon, dash, "that is," "in other words" | match the explanation that follows |
| "unlike," "although," "but," "rather than" | be the opposite of the contrasted idea |
| "because," "since," "so," "thus" | be logically connected to the cause or effect |
| "and," "also," "similarly" | match the tone of the nearby idea |

Rule: **fill the blank with your own word first**, even a rough one like "quiet" or "not lasting." Then find the choice that means that.

### "As used in the text"

These questions ask what a word means in this sentence, not what it means most often. Read the whole sentence, then substitute a simple word that fits the sentence's logic. The choice that fits the sentence is the answer, even if it is an unusual meaning of the word.

## Worked example 1 (easy)

> Unlike the crowded downtown market, the farm stand on Route 9 was ______, with only a single table and a handwritten price list.

A) bustling
B) modest
C) expensive
D) famous

1. "Unlike the crowded downtown market" is a contrast clue. The blank means "not crowded, small."
2. "Only a single table" confirms: small and simple.
3. A means busy, the opposite of what is needed.
4. C and D have no support; prices and fame are not discussed.
5. **B**, "modest," means small and simple. Answer: B.

## Worked example 2 (SAT-level)

> Early reviewers dismissed the composer's first symphony as ______, noting that nearly every melody seemed borrowed from works by more established composers. Only decades later did critics recognize the inventive ways she had rearranged those familiar themes.

A) chaotic
B) imitative
C) incomplete
D) ambitious

1. The clue follows the comma: "nearly every melody seemed borrowed." Your own word: "copied."
2. A, "chaotic," means disorderly; borrowing melodies is not disorder.
3. C, "incomplete," is not supported; nothing is described as unfinished.
4. D, "ambitious," is positive, but the reviewers "dismissed" the work, which is a negative judgment.
5. **B**, "imitative," means copying others. It fits both the borrowing and the negative tone. Answer: B.

The last sentence is a distraction: it describes later critics, not the early reviewers whose view the blank describes.

## Common traps

- **Picking the word that "sounds smart."** Hard vocabulary is not automatically right. Many correct answers are simple words.
- **Right direction, wrong degree.** If the text says the effect was small, "devastating" is wrong even though it is about effect size.
- **Answering for the wrong person.** In "critics said X, but later scholars found Y," make sure the blank matches the right group.
- **Ignoring tone.** If the sentence is disapproving, a positive word cannot fit, even if its meaning is close.
- **Choosing a word that is related but not precise.** "Withstand" and "resist" can both relate to strong winds; check which one the sentence structure actually needs.

## The fast way (and when to use Desmos)

Desmos is not used for Reading and Writing questions. The fastest approach is:

1. Cover the choices and read the full text, including what comes *after* the blank.
2. Find the clue (contrast, restatement, cause-effect) and predict your own word.
3. Eliminate choices that are the wrong direction (positive vs. negative) first.
4. Among the rest, pick the one that matches your prediction most precisely.

If you don't know a choice's meaning, judge the choices you *do* know. If all three known words are clearly wrong, the unknown word is the answer. These questions should take 45 to 60 seconds.
