## What this skill is

Rhetorical Synthesis questions show a bulleted list of a student's research notes, then state a goal: "The student wants to emphasize..." or "...to introduce X to an audience unfamiliar with it." You pick the sentence that "most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal." Every choice is accurate to the notes. Only one does what the goal asks.

## Learn the idea

**Try this first.** The goal is to explain how the two cities differ, while also noting one thing they share. Notes: City A has a river. City B has a river. City B is larger. What should the answer include?

**Let's work it out.**

1. Read the goal first. It asks for two things: a difference and a similarity. Why read the goal first? The notes are a pile of facts, and the goal decides which facts you use.
2. Find the difference in the notes. Only one fact separates the cities: City B is larger. A river does not separate them, since both have one.
3. Find the similarity. Both cities have a river. That fact fits the goal's second part.
4. Combine the two. A correct answer names the difference and the shared feature together. An answer with only the river, or only the size, misses part of the goal.

**Your turn.** Goal: compare the two plans' monthly costs. Notes: Plan A costs \$20 a month. Plan B costs \$15 a month plus a \$5 setup fee. Which facts does the goal need?

**Answer:** The goal needs the two monthly costs: \$20 for Plan A and \$15 for Plan B. The setup fee is a one-time cost, so it doesn't belong in a monthly comparison unless the goal asks for it.

## Formulas and rules

**Key rules**

| Rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| Goal first | Read the goal before the notes. |
| Eliminate on the goal | Most choices are accurate, so eliminate the ones that miss the goal. |
| Multi-part goals | Each part of the goal needs its own support in the answer. |

| Goal word | The correct sentence must... |
|---|---|
| emphasize a size / amount / extent | include the specific number or scale |
| explain why / the significance | include a reason ("because," "so that," "which allows") |
| compare / contrast / differ | mention **both** items and how they relate |
| similarity | state something true of both |
| introduce to an unfamiliar audience | name it and say what it is |
| present a study / its findings | include the researchers' method or result as asked |

Rule: **turn the goal into a checklist**, then check each choice against it.

### More goals

- **Refute a claim:** the correct sentence must present information that contradicts the claim.
- **Generalize:** it must make a statement about the whole group and support it with a specific example from the notes.
- **Describe a format:** it must name the form and say what it does, such as its length or rhythm.
- **Specify:** it must give concrete details, like a date, a place, or a number, from the notes.

### Goals that ask for a specific kind of detail

- **Indicate or identify:** list the specific items from the notes that answer the question, such as particular regions.
- **Provide an overview:** briefly describe the whole program or topic and its main parts.
- **Place in context:** say when or where something happened, relative to a person's life or a larger event.
- **Give a specific example:** choose one concrete case, not a general statement.

## Worked example 1 (easy)

> - The Kessler Bridge crosses the Tamar River.
> - It opened in 1932.
> - It is 1,850 meters long.
> - It was the longest bridge in the region when it opened.

*The student wants to emphasize the length of the Kessler Bridge. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?*

A) The Kessler Bridge, which crosses the Tamar River, opened in 1932.
B) At 1,850 meters, the Kessler Bridge was the longest bridge in the region when it opened.
C) The Kessler Bridge opened in 1932 and crosses the Tamar River.
D) In 1932, a new bridge opened over the Tamar River.

Checklist: mentions length, ideally with emphasis.

1. A, C, and D are true but never mention length.
2. **B** gives the number and stresses it ("longest in the region"). Answer: B.

## Worked example 2 (SAT-level)

> - Saffron and turmeric are both spices used to color food yellow.
> - Saffron comes from the dried stigmas of a crocus flower.
> - Turmeric comes from the dried root of a plant in the ginger family.
> - Saffron is far more expensive because each flower yields only three stigmas, which are picked by hand.

*The student wants to contrast the sources of the two spices. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?*

A) Saffron and turmeric are both used to give food a yellow color.
B) Saffron is expensive because its stigmas must be picked by hand.
C) While saffron comes from the dried stigmas of a crocus flower, turmeric comes from the dried root of a ginger-family plant.
D) Turmeric, a spice made from a dried root, is a member of the ginger family.

Checklist: (1) both spices, (2) their sources, (3) a contrast.

1. A gives a similarity, not a contrast.
2. B mentions only saffron, and it is about price.
3. D mentions only turmeric.
4. **C** names both sources and contrasts them with "While." Answer: C.

## Common traps

- **Choosing the most detailed sentence.** More facts is not better; the right facts are.
- **True but off-goal.** This is the main trap. Every wrong choice is accurate to the notes.
- **One-sided comparisons.** A contrast goal needs both items in the sentence.
- **Missing half of a two-part goal.** "Difference *and* similarity" requires both.
- **Ignoring the audience.** If the reader is unfamiliar, a choice that uses a term without explaining it fails.

## The fast way (and when to use Desmos)

Desmos is not used for Reading and Writing questions. The fastest approach is:

1. **Skip the notes at first.** Read the goal in the question stem.
2. Write a mini-checklist (e.g., "both spices + sources + contrast").
3. Check each choice against the checklist and eliminate any that miss a part.
4. Glance at the notes only if two choices both seem to fit.

Most synthesis questions take 30 to 45 seconds with this approach, even though the notes look long.
