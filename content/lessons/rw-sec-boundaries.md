## What this skill is

Boundaries questions ask you to choose the punctuation that correctly connects (or separates) parts of a sentence. The stem is always "Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?" The choices are usually the same words with different punctuation: commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, periods, or nothing at all.

## Key ideas

- An **independent clause** (IC) has a subject and verb and can stand alone as a sentence: "The museum opened in 1890."
- A **dependent clause** cannot stand alone: "because the museum opened," "which the museum displays."
- Two independent clauses can't be joined with just a comma (a *comma splice*) or with nothing (a *run-on*).
- A **nonessential element** (extra information) must be set off with matching marks on both sides: two commas, two dashes, or two parentheses.
- Don't put punctuation between a subject and its verb, between a verb and its object, or before a list that the sentence's grammar leads straight into.

## Formulas and rules

| Joining... | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| IC + IC | IC**.** IC · IC**;** IC · IC**,** and IC · IC**:** IC (if the second explains the first) | IC**,** IC · IC IC |
| IC + dependent clause | IC dependent · IC**,** which... | IC**;** dependent · IC**.** Which... |
| IC + list or explanation | IC**:** list | subject verb**:** list ("include: X") |
| Nonessential element | , X , · — X — | , X — (mismatched) |
| Subject + verb | no punctuation | subject**,** verb |

Key facts:

- **Semicolon = period.** Both sides must be independent clauses.
- **Colon** must follow a complete independent clause. What comes after it explains, lists, or illustrates.
- **Conjunctive adverbs** ("however," "therefore," "for example") are not conjunctions. Between two ICs they need a semicolon or period before them: "...rain**;** however, ..."

## Worked example 1 (easy)

> The orchestra rehearsed for six weeks before the ______ the performance sold out within an hour.

A) concert,
B) concert
C) concert;
D) concert, and,

1. Split the sentence: "The orchestra rehearsed for six weeks before the concert" is an IC. "The performance sold out within an hour" is also an IC.
2. A is a comma splice. B is a run-on.
3. D adds a comma after "and," which is unnecessary.
4. **C**: a semicolon correctly joins two ICs. Answer: C.

## Worked example 2 (SAT-level)

> The town's oldest building, a stone mill constructed in ______ still uses its original water wheel to grind grain for local bakeries.

A) 1782;
B) 1782
C) 1782—
D) 1782,

1. Find the core sentence: "The town's oldest building ... still uses its original water wheel."
2. "a stone mill constructed in 1782" is extra information. It opens with a comma after "building," so it must close with a **comma**.
3. A: a semicolon would cut the subject off from its verb.
4. B: no closing mark leaves the nonessential element unbalanced.
5. C: a dash doesn't match the opening comma.
6. **D** closes the pair. Answer: D.

## Common traps

- **Mismatched pairs.** Opening with a comma and closing with a dash (or vice versa).
- **Comma before "however" between two ICs.** "...late, however, the..." is a splice. Use "late; however, the..."
- **Colon after an incomplete clause.** "The recipe needs: flour" is wrong. "The recipe needs three things: flour..." is right.
- **Semicolon before a dependent clause.** "...the plant; which grows..." is wrong; "which" can't start an independent clause.
- **Extra commas.** A comma between a subject and its verb ("The result of the study, was clear") is always wrong.

## The fast way (and when to use Desmos)

Desmos is not used for Reading and Writing questions. The fastest approach is:

1. Look at the choices to see what's changing (period vs. comma vs. semicolon, etc.).
2. Find the boundary and ask: **is the part before it an independent clause? Is the part after it?**
3. Both independent → period, semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so).
4. Not both → usually a comma or nothing. Check for a nonessential element that needs a matching mark.
5. Eliminate choices that are grammatically identical: if a period and a semicolon would both work, neither is the answer.

Aim for 30–45 seconds per question.
